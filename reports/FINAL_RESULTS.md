# DAO governance pipeline: final results

Summary of everything the pipeline produced, written for the paper. Every number below is generated by
`run_all.py` and is also available as a LaTeX macro in `reports/paper_numbers.tex`
(`\input{paper_numbers}`; key macro names are listed in Section 8). Do not type numbers by hand; if the
pipeline is rerun, the macros update. Labels: **Finding** = computed result; **Interpretation** = our
reading of it; **Caveat** = what limits it.

## 1. Data and sample

**Sources.** Governance data from the Tally (Cactus) GraphQL API: organizations, governors, tokens,
delegates (up to 500 per governor, sorted by voting power), and all proposals with quorum, vote totals and
voter counts. Market data from CryptoCompare: daily USD prices (`histoday`) and a current market snapshot
(`pricemultifull`). Benchmark: BTC. All raw responses are cached with collection timestamps.

**Dates.** Governance snapshot and market snapshot collected 2026-10-05. Returns and events cover
January 2021 to September 2026.

**Unit of observation.** A DAO is a governance token contract on a mainnet chain. Organizations that share
a token (e.g. successive governor versions of the same protocol) are merged into one DAO.

**Sample construction** (`reports/tables/sample_funnel_summary.csv`; every exclusion with its reason in
`reports/sample_funnel.csv`):

| Step | Rule | DAOs remaining |
|---|---|---|
| U1 | all Tally organizations | 7,864 organizations |
| U2 | at least one proposal | 3,234 |
| U3 | not a test, mock, staging or deprecated organization | 2,295 |
| U3b | governance token on a mainnet chain | 743 |
| U4 | fungible (ERC20) governance token | 680 |
| U5 | one DAO per token contract | 636 DAOs |
| U6 | ticker found in CryptoCompare with a matching project name | 80 |
| U6b | one token contract per ticker | 69 |
| U7 | a governor uses the DAO token | 69 |
| U8 | match verified by supply check (42) or accepted with a written reason (12) | **54** |

Of the 54 DAOs: 46 have daily prices (8 tokens have no USD market in CryptoCompare), 45 have at least 12
months of returns, 39 are on Ethereum mainnet, and 25 are active (at least one concluded proposal in the 12
months before collection).

**Token matching.** CryptoCompare no longer publishes contract addresses, so tokens are matched by ticker,
checked by project name, and verified by comparing the on-chain total supply (Tally) with CryptoCompare's
supply (ratio within 0.5-2). Ticker collisions are common: the same ticker often belongs to an unrelated
project in CryptoCompare (e.g. LEND is the old Aave/ETHLend token, not Lendefi DAO; STRK is Starknet,
not Strike; KITE is a different project with a 2 billion USD market cap, not HAI). Author decisions on
disputed matches are in `config_decisions/token_match_decisions.csv`.

**Governor selection.** When a DAO has several governors, the one used for delegates and quorum is the
primary governor with the largest delegated voting power; ties are broken by the most recent concluded
proposal, then by proposal count; test governors are excluded. Proposals (events) come from all governors
of the DAO token. Tally lists no main Optimism governor, so OP is represented by a minor governor.

## 2. Variables

| Variable | Definition |
|---|---|
| HHI | sum of squared shares of the top-100 delegates; shares relative to the delegated votes of the same governor |
| Top-k share | share of delegated votes held by the k largest delegates (k = 1, 5, 10) |
| Nakamoto coefficient | fewest delegates holding a majority of delegated votes |
| Q | fewest top delegates whose votes reach the quorum; quorum = median of the last 5 concluded proposals, excluding passed proposals whose votes cast are below their quorum (data errors) |
| Participation | mean unique voters over the 20 most recent concluded proposals / number of delegates (missing if above 1) |
| Turnout | mean votes cast over the same proposals / token total supply |
| Size | FDV of live tokens = mean price in September 2026 x total supply, tokens with trading volume only (main); CryptoCompare market cap; FDV averaged over the token's trading life |
| Returns | monthly log return (month-end close); BTC-adjusted = token minus BTC |
| Abnormal return | daily token log return minus BTC log return; CAR = sum over [-5, +5] around the proposal end date |

No value is imputed. Missing values stay missing with a recorded reason.

## 3. Descriptive facts (governance structure)

**Finding: delegated voting power is highly concentrated.** Across 52 DAOs with delegate data: median HHI
0.25; the largest delegate holds a median 42% of delegated votes; the top 10 hold a median 98%; the
median Nakamoto coefficient is 2 (two delegates control a majority).

**Finding: quorum capture.** Of the 38 DAOs whose quorum is reachable, in **24 (63%)** the single largest
delegate's current voting power alone exceeds the quorum (Q = 1); among active DAOs, 14 of 23 (61%).
The median quorum is 24% of delegated votes and the median top-1 share 38%. Examples: Aave, ENS,
Compound, Ondo, Gitcoin, Threshold, Radworks, PoolTogether.
*Caveat:* current voting power vs the recent quorum; passing a proposal also needs a majority of votes cast.

**Finding: unreachable quorums.** In 11 DAOs the quorum exceeds all currently delegated votes: quorums are
2-25% of supply, but only 0.0002-8% of supply is delegated. These DAOs cannot pass a proposal with today's
delegation. Remaining Q statuses: no delegation data 2, implausible quorum units 2 (rebasing token; minor
governor), no quorum 1.

**Finding: dormancy.** In 18 of 51 DAOs, more votes were once cast in a single proposal than are delegated
today. A snapshot of today's delegation therefore understates historical participation for about a third
of the sample.

Figures: `reports/figures/figure2_governance_distributions` (HHI, top-10 share, participation, Q) and
`fig_market_distributions` (live-token FDV, average monthly return, delegates, turnout).

## 4. H1: governance and DAO size

**Test.** Spearman correlations (with 95% CIs and power) and OLS (HC3) of log size on governance measures,
for three size measures; bootstrap (1,000 reps, seed 42) for log size on log HHI.

**Finding: no measure of concentration is related to size in the main sample.** Live-token FDV, N = 28:
ln HHI rho = -0.12 (95% CI -0.48 to 0.26, p 0.53); top-1 share -0.07 (p 0.71); top-10 share -0.33
(p 0.09); Nakamoto coefficient +0.14 (p 0.47). Bootstrap: 81% of slopes negative, p 0.37.

**Finding: size is strongly related to the size of the delegate base.** ln(delegates) rho = 0.54 (p < 0.01);
ln(holders) rho = 0.61 (p < 0.01).

**Finding: the concentration-size relation that appears in larger samples is explained by the delegate
base.** Life-average FDV, N = 44: ln HHI rho = -0.29 (p 0.05), top-10 share -0.41 (p 0.01), and OLS ln HHI
-0.92 (SE 0.38, p 0.02). Adding ln(delegates): ln HHI -0.10 (p 0.81), ln(delegates) 0.45 (p < 0.01).
With controls for delegates, token age and chain, ln HHI is insignificant for every size measure
(p 0.47-0.62).

**Finding: measures divided by the delegate count correlate with size mechanically.** Participation
(rho -0.43, p 0.03) and Q / delegates (rho -0.60, p 0.01) are significant, but their denominator, the
delegate count, is itself strongly correlated with size.

Figure: `fig_h1_scatter` (life-average FDV, N = 44): size vs HHI (slope -0.92), size vs delegates (0.47),
and the partial regression of size on HHI given delegates (-0.10, flat).

**Interpretation.** Larger DAOs are less concentrated because they have more delegates; given the delegate
base, concentration is unrelated to size. Size goes with breadth of participation, not with the
distribution of power among participants.

**Caveat.** With N = 28 only correlations above about 0.5 in absolute value are detected with 80% power;
moderate effects (e.g. 0.25) cannot be ruled out. Report CIs, not only p-values. Active DAOs only (N = 11-23)
point in the same direction and do not change the conclusion.

## 5. H2: governance and returns

**Test.** OLS (HC3) of the average monthly log return (DAOs with at least 12 months) on governance measures;
same with BTC-adjusted returns; Spearman correlations.

**Finding: governance does not predict average returns.** Models M1-M3 (N = 29-43): no governance variable
is significant for raw or BTC-adjusted returns (e.g. ln HHI 0.014, p 0.21, N = 43).

**Caveat.** The specification that adds size (M4) has N = 19 and is not interpreted; size there is
measured at the end of the sample and is mechanically related to past returns.

## 6. H3: returns around governance votes

**Test.** Event = end of voting of a concluded proposal; window [-5, +5] days; abnormal return = token minus
BTC. 2,748 proposals collected; 1,806 have a complete price window (35 DAOs, 1,391 distinct event dates,
January 2021 to September 2026). Inference: SE clustered by DAO and by DAO and calendar month, DAO-level
tests, placebo windows (1,000 reps).

**Finding: raw CAR is negative.** CAR[-5, +5] = -1.89% (proposal-weighted); 61% of proposals have a negative
CAR. Raw CAR is significant under all clustering schemes, but it includes the general underperformance of
DAO tokens against BTC.

**Finding: excess over each DAO's own baseline is small and not robust.** Baseline = mean CAR of the DAO's
own event-free 11-day windows.

| Sample | Events | Excess CAR | p (cluster DAO) | p (DAO x month) | p (DAO level) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| All proposals | 1,806 | -1.70% | 0.07 | 0.09 | 0.09 |
| Distinct event dates | 1,391 | -1.91% | 0.04 | 0.06 | 0.10 |
| Non-overlapping event dates | 736 | -1.46% | 0.14 | 0.17 | 0.19 |

**Finding: event days and other days look alike.** Mean daily abnormal return -0.240% on event-window days
vs -0.226% on other days (p 0.88).

**Interpretation.** Point estimates suggest token prices fall about 1.5-2% relative to their own normal
behaviour around votes, but the evidence is weak and disappears with non-overlapping windows and clustering.

**Methodological note.** Placebo tests that treat windows as independent give small p-values (0.00-0.07),
because votes of different DAOs cluster in the same market periods. Comparing a proposal-weighted CAR with
a DAO-weighted placebo (or the reverse) can produce opposite conclusions; the own-baseline comparison avoids
the weighting choice. Figures: `reports/figures/figure3_placebo`; `fig_h3_event_path` (mean abnormal return
by day and cumulative path, raw and over the own baseline, 95% bands from a bootstrap over DAOs).

## 7. H4: market factor

**Test.** Pooled monthly panel of token returns on BTC returns; nested models on a common sample;
SE clustered by DAO with t(G-1) critical values and wild cluster bootstrap (999 reps).

**Finding: crypto-market risk dominates.** All 45 DAOs, 1,853 DAO-months: BTC beta 0.91 (SE 0.06),
R2 0.14; Spearman between token and BTC returns 0.51.
Figure: `fig_h4_btc` (monthly token vs BTC returns; 4 points beyond +/-2 not shown).

**Finding: governance adds almost nothing.** Common sample of 27 DAOs (1,108 DAO-months): R2 0.259 with BTC
only, 0.262 adding ln HHI and ln(delegates), 0.272 adding size. Without the size control, ln HHI is not
significant (wild-bootstrap p 0.30) and ln(delegates) is borderline (0.006 per log unit; cluster p 0.06,
wild p 0.05).

**Caveat.** Conditional on end-of-sample size, ln HHI becomes significant (wild p 0.04); this is not
interpreted because the size control is mechanically related to past returns.

## 8. Methodological lessons for empirical DAO research

These came up while building the dataset and are useful in their own right for the SoK.
1. **Ticker collisions.** Matching governance tokens to market data by ticker produces wrong projects
   for several of the largest tokens. Verify by name and on-chain supply; contract addresses are no
   longer available from CryptoCompare.
2. **Test and duplicate organizations.** Most Tally organizations with proposals are tests, testnet
   deployments, forks or copies of real tokens (2,295 non-test organizations, but only 743 with a mainnet
   token and 636 distinct token contracts).
3. **Governor choice.** Several governors per DAO report identical delegated votes; an arbitrary choice
   picks deprecated or test governors. Use activity to break ties.
4. **Consistent denominators.** Concentration shares must use the delegated votes of the same governor as
   the delegate list; mixing organization-wide delegates with one governor's total yields HHI above 1.
5. **Snapshot vs history.** Today's delegate counts and delegated votes are compared with historical
   voting; ratios above 1 (participation, turnout, dormancy) reveal shrinking delegation, not errors.
6. **Units.** Rebasing tokens report votes in internal units; quorum values can contain data errors
   (a passed proposal with votes below its quorum). Check quorum against supply.
7. **Stale market data.** Many small governance tokens have no recent trades; market caps and carried-
   forward prices of dead tokens must be filtered (by last update and trading volume).
8. **Event-study weighting.** Proposal-heavy DAOs dominate proposal-weighted averages; overlapping windows
   and common calendar shocks require clustered or non-overlapping inference.

## 9. Limitations

- Small samples: 28 DAOs with live-token FDV, 35 in the event study, 27 in the H4 governance panel.
- Governance is a single snapshot (October 2026); returns span 2021-2026.
- Size in H2 and H4 controls is measured at the end of the sample.
- Coverage is limited to on-chain governance indexed by Tally and tokens traded in CryptoCompare;
  off-chain (Snapshot) voting is not included.
- Results are associations, not causal effects.
